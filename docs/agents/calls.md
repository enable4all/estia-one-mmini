# Calls and voice

## Layout

- `voice` (audio type, voice-service client and its speech and turn detectors (`vad`, `turn`, `detector_state(part=…)`), spoken text without stage directions (`without_stage_directions`), `afconvert`)
- `call_audio` (a call's audio over the call helper's socket: frame in, frame out, end of turn (`TurnEnd`, one per call like `Barge`: the full pause, or sooner when Smart Turn, asked once a pause at the short pause about the last 8 s, is sure (p >= 0.8: at once) or probably sure (p >= 0.5: at 1.5 s), 3 failed or late answers in a row the full pause for the rest of the call; and the pause by speech, where a loud frame counts as pause once the speech detector rated every chunk it falls in as noise (rule R4, p < 0.1), so a turn never ends later than by loudness; no judge and no detector: today's listen, byte for byte; each listen's end kept, edited by calls.py, for the call's two end lines; `CallAudio.watch`: the caller going on after an early end), barge-in by loudness or speech: `Barge`), `calls` (voice calls: answer the allowed user, place a call for a reminder, the laid-back conversation, the end of a turn (one `TurnEnd` a call on the barge-in stream, the watch, the hold: `goes_on`), her own voice, summary, the note after every call she answered), `voice_ready` (code's note "My voice is ready: you can call me now." after a long load or a call that rang while the voice was not ready), `voice_download` (a language's warm-up, and while jane's model of a language downloads, no line in it: `/language`'s and `/babble`'s notes, a voice note's text answer with its note, a call rung out with its text, then "Her German voice is ready.")
- `tools/voice/`: the Swift voice service (WhisperKit on the Neural Engine; Pocket TTS, her only voice (owner, 3 Nov 2026: "Pocket TTS only" under Rules), for English in the voices heart, jane, eve, vera, alba and michael on the Mac's model (`PocketVoices`: the 100M beside Gemma 4 26B, the 18M beside 12B, which has no vera: heart says her lines there; heart's files, one a model, ship in the repository, `Sources/voice-service/Voices`), and for Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, German and French in jane (each language's own model: `PocketLanguages.swift`, jane whatever voice a request names; Korean, Polish, Russian and Japanese have no voice: 409 `no_voice`), its release program (beside the voice service in her release) run once a line on the CPU on a queue of its own (`Pocket.swift`, VoiceCore `PocketTTS`); and Silero VAD through FluidAudio for barge-in, on the CPU only; 127.0.0.1:8150), its core tested with Swift Testing.
- `tools/call-tunnel/` (AGPL-3.0-only, see its `LICENSE-NOTICE.md`): build script, pins and local patch for our signal-call-tunnel (the call helper signal-cli starts per call) and the JVM signal-cli installer. `build.sh` resets its own clone before it applies `local.patch` and writes a stamp beside the helper it built (`<helper>.stamp`: `PR5_HEAD`, `RINGRTC` and local.patch's sha256, a line each, `config.tunnel_stamp`); setup's calls step counts the helper built, in the checkout and in her release, only with the stamp of the checkout's own sources, deploy copies the stamp with the helper, and doctor's "Call helper" ! names one built from other sources or before the stamp (`config.tunnel_stamp_differs`; second deep scan, signal-06: a later Estia's local.patch was never built).

  - A line Pocket cannot say (a failed or late run, a refused file, a model not downloaded yet, a voice resting after failures) is said by no other voice. ## Gotchas

- The Neural Engine compiles a model on its first use: about 15 minutes for Whisper large-v3 turbo, cached per process name and macOS build (`~/Library/Caches/voice-service/`), so again after a macOS update. The voice service warms up at start and says "loading" until then. It compiles again per user: about 15 minutes after the move into her own user (live, 13 Oct), although the move copies the cache. Calls go unanswered meanwhile (hence the ready note).
- WhisperKit stops a window's seek `windowClipTime` (1 s by default) before the audio's end, so with its default it decodes nothing in a clip of 1 s or less: a call's turn of one short word ("yes", "stop", "hello", her name alone) came back as no words, "noise heard as words" in the log (live, 23 Oct: four turns of 0.7-0.8 s in standby; the voice log's "0 chars in 0.00 s"). `whisperOptions` sets it to 0: our audio is at most one window (30 s; longer audio is cut at pauses by WhisperKit's VAD chunker, which keeps its own 1 s, so no chunk is that short), so nothing is clipped from its end. The `WhisperStart` real-model test checks a word under 1 s both ways, so a WhisperKit upgrade that changes it shows. The start's warm-up (a second of silence) uses a call's options too (`warmUpOptions`; review 0.9.7, C20): with WhisperKit's defaults it ran no encoder and no decoder (measured 25 Oct on the CPU: 0 and 0, now 1 and 5), so the first turn after a start paid the first use; `WhisperStart` checks both.
- VoiceCore's `cleanTranscript` erases a transcript of only "thank you", "thanks." or "bye." (Whisper's habit on silence) unless the request is a call's (`?call=1`, `TranscriptionRequest.call`): every call transcription sends it (`VoiceClient.transcribe(call=True)`: `_hear` and the one after a barge-in), so a caller's bare "Bye." ends the call and a bare thanks counts (review I23: erased, she waited for the check-in); `is_noise_transcript` still takes a lone "bye", "thanks" or "thank you" on less than `NOISE_VOICED_S` of sustained voice for noise; voice notes keep the old list (a voice note of only "Thanks." is unclear); an older voice service ignores the flag. - English speech (VoiceCore `SpokenEnglish`, Kokoro's, and Pocket's before `PocketText`) is one list of passes in one order (`SpokenEnglish.order`, pinned by a test): a pass that runs early owns its text (times take their colon, dates their numbers, units their amounts; symbols run just before the numbers are read, since their rules look at the digits). Chords (`ChordSpeller`, the chord speech audit, 18 Oct): a run joins on spaces, commas, dashes, bars, arrows, semicolons (the bridge says a table's bar lines as "; "), slashes with a space before them (beat slashes), "…", "‖", "⏸" and "𝄐", each gap said as a comma (a semicolon or a slash only next to a chord or a mark said: "Take route A; B is closed" stays); a chart's marks are run members said as words among chords or in a sentence about music (`ChordSpeller.Mark`, one pattern the ten languages share: "N.C." "no chord" among chords, and alone in a sentence about music only where a chart puts it (`ChordSpeller.chartSpot`: after a colon, a music word or, in English, "on", "is" or "with"; never "NC" alone or a place, "lives in N.C.", "Raleigh, N.C.", "de N.C.": the review, 18 Oct); "%" "repeat" only between chords; a repeat after a chord said, a section's name ("Chorus x2") or a bracket at a clause's end (`ChordSpeller.endsClause`: "Buy strings (x2) for the guitar" is a quantity) "twice", "three times"); three chords or more in a row with sheet gaps alone between them (no comma, dash or word) that are their sentence alone (only marks, punctuation and bars around them: `standsAlone`) make a chord row, so its bare letters are major chords and an opening "A" is the chord, never the article, unless the letters run in alphabet order ("The A B C of") or the sentence is about notes ("The notes are G B D F"); inside a sentence such letters stay letters ("Standard tuning is E A D G B E", "Vitamins A C E": the review, 18 Oct), and a table row of answers ("| A | C | B |") is a known gap; "-" right after the root before a number is minor (ambiguous: "the A-7 south" stays; with a sharp or flat strong, "F#-7", in the ten languages alike: the review, 18 Oct), "CmMaj7" and brackets with "maj7", "add9", "no3" or alterations are strong.
- Speech in the other ten languages (VoiceCore `SpokenLanguage`: Supertonic-3's, and Pocket's jane in six, then her guard) works the same way: one list of passes per language in one order (`SpokenLanguage.order`, pinned by a test), each language's words, tables and context lists in its pack (`Languages/Pack_<code>.swift`), numbers from Foundation's spell-out in the full locale (`Cardinals`: soft hyphens out, pt_PT's "mil milhões", ru's "ноль целых"; fr with spaces for hyphens, the live check), forms from hand tables (`Inflect`: last-word gender, the pl and ru plural rule, ordinals, pl and ru genitives and years), chords from their own parser (`ChordWords`), spelled only in a music context as in English (a strong chord anywhere; "G7", "C#", "Am" with the pack's music words, a strong chord, a run of three or a run that is the whole sentence; else "C#" is "C sharp"; ja and ko spell as before); every gap but a comma between two chords said is the pack's `dash` (", ", ja "、"), and the gaps are English's; es, it, pt, fr, nl, de, pl and ru also read "C-7", a minor chord with a major seventh, alterations (the sign by its name, then the number) and "(no3)" (`Style.forms`: a bracket with a bass or a sus keeps the reading from before, `plainCandidate`), and a chart's marks in their own words (`Style.marks`); ja and ko have neither, so their candidate is the one from before. A new rule goes in its pass, with rows in `LanguageTable` (`SpokenLanguageTests.swift`), the rows that must stay as written too; every row runs through `spokenInput`. Shared patterns are `static let`s; a language's own (month names, its ordinals, its currencies) are compiled once, when its reader is first made. ja and ko keep the digits Supertonic-3 reads right and rewrite only the failing pieces; letter classes next to a number are Latin and Cyrillic only, so kana and Hangul glued to it never block a match. Foundation's `Unit` class is why the units enum is `MeasureUnit`. `ChordWords`' tail numbers end where the digits do and it holds at most 8 parts (review 0.9.7, C7: "C", 34 digits and a letter took 3.4 s in every language, exponential in the digits, while the engine queue waited; `ChordPatternTime` times such texts), and the spoken text is worked out off the engines' actor, before the engine queue (`Server.speak` hands it to `Engines.speak`), so text work never holds a call's listening.
- Outgoing calls (signal-cli 0.14.9 `startCall`, experimental upstream): the `RINGING_OUTGOING` event comes before `startCall`'s reply, so the call id is not known yet when it arrives (`CallManager._dial` buffers events); `CONNECTING` only means it reached the phone. The end reasons (`ring_timeout`, `remote_hangup`, `remote_busy`, …) and pipe-mode audio on an outgoing call still need the live test (docs/e2e-checklist.md, 83-86).
